The new tests were observed failing before the relevant implementation. Thirty-nine tests were added beyond the 102-test merged baseline. Fault scenarios include:

- An acknowledged registration survives hard process termination without relying on shutdown hooks.
- Offline browser/relay restoration retains desired targets and retries rather than forgetting them.
- Completion receipts survive restart until ACK; unregister survives restart without reactivation.
- A failed watcher cannot abort polling or cleanup of its siblings.
- A stale poll snapshot cannot execute a withdrawn generation; unregister fences in-flight work before returning.
- Concurrent registration/polling cannot create a second binding for the same generation.
- Registry control reads and health remain responsive while another request is incomplete.
- A broken durable store stops watcher effects before execution and is visible in health.
- A continuous stream of unrelated CDP events cannot keep a request alive indefinitely.
- A late response is not accepted, a timed-out mutation is not resent, and the next request gets a fresh timeout budget.
- Initial attachment failure closes its socket; stale-socket cleanup cannot discard a verified replacement.
- Unknown state, human gates and uncertain delivery remain non-writing states, with explicit diagnostics.
- Durable managed restoration rejects unbrokered `--legacy-direct-send`; explicit single-conversation legacy mode remains separate.

These are controlled software/process tests. They are not a claim of unlimited uptime or a new successful real ChatGPT prompt delivery.

## Live deployment boundary: intentionally not switched

The new wheel was installed and tested in a stable release-specific virtual environment. The production registry on port 9235 was NOT stopped or replaced.

The decisive pre-handover reread found that the production inventory had changed from two to three active registrations during this task. Another workflow was still using the registration endpoint. The old in-memory registry has no atomic freeze/export/handover interface; an uncoordinated stop could lose a concurrent registration or receipt. An earlier saved inventory is not authoritative enough to override that observation.

A separate legacy compatibility process also has three active registrations and its own database and scheduled task. None of those registrations, processes, task definitions, environments or databases was altered. They were not silently copied into the managed Sidecar runtime.

The safe remaining handover requires a controlled registration-quiescence boundary, a fresh active/receipt inventory, an exact old-process/package rollback receipt, and restoration into the new store before activating the replacement. The new runtime must then be verified through its actual module/store/instance identity and repeated polling timestamps, not a launch ACK. Do not point the new store at the incompatible legacy database.

No browser extension code changed, so no extension reload or browser shutdown was performed. One managed target was observed in authoritative uncertain-delivery state; forced continuation would violate the delivery invariant, not repair availability.

## Unclosed or explicitly excluded gates

- Production process handover and operating-system restart ownership have not been changed or validated for the new release.
- No new real-browser delivery/fault/reload acceptance was forced onto active user conversations.
- No independent agent audit completed: available worker routes did not produce a usable reviewer. The adversarial regression tests are self-review evidence, not independent review.
- PC1-local, unpushed registration-authority changes were not accessible and are not claimed merged. The existing trusted-local-operator boundary remains; persistence does not manufacture requester authorization.
- Sidecar reconciliation of an uncertain delivery is a separate owner-side operation. Watchdog must expose that state and preserve the stop boundary, not clear it or bypass its single writer.
